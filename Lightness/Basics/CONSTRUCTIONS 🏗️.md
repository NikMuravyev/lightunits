### Definitions

📍[@goldbergConstructionsConstructionGrammar1995]:
C is a construction iff C is a form-meaning pair <Fi , Si> such that some aspect of Fi or some aspect of Si is not strictly predictable from C’s component parts or from other previously established constructions. 

A Russian translation in [@rahilinaLingvistikaKonstrukciy2010]
Под конструкцией понимается «языковое выражение, у которого есть аспект плана выражения или плана содержания, не выводимый из значения или формы составных частей»

📍[@croftRadicalConstructionGrammar2001]:
The general definition of a construction in cognitive linguistics is as a conventional symbolic unit... Roughly, a construction is an entrenched routine (‘unit’), that is generally used in the speech community (‘conventional’), and involves a pairing of form and meaning (‘symbolic')

📍[@haspelmathWhatConstruction2023]:
A construction is a conventional schema for creating or motivating well-formed expressions in which there is at least one open slot that can be filled by one of several expressions belonging to the same form-class.

📍Original Fillmore's definition (1988): 
Constructions on our view are much like the nuclear family (mother plus daughters) subtrees admitted by phrase structure rules, EXCEPT that 
(1) constructions need not be limited to a mother and her daughters,but may span wider ranges of the sententialtree; (2) constructions may specify, not only syntactic, but also lexical, semantic, and pragmatic information; 
(3) lexical items, being mentionable in syntactic constructions, may be viewed, in many cases at least, as constructions themselves; and
(4) constructions may be idiomatic in the sense that a large construction may specify a semantics (and/orpragmatics) that is distinct from what might be calculated from the associated semantics of the set of smaller constructions that could be used to build the same morphosyntactic object
### Constructions and typology

- Typological extention of CxG is suggested by W. Croft in his work on RCxG 
- Croft's approach was criticized by H. Boas for being too coarse-grained
- H. Boas instead proposes a bottom-up approach that starts with a detailed comparison of constructional equivalents between only two languages, cf. [@boasContrastiveStudiesConstruction2010] 
- A new practice-related definition of a construction is proposed by M. Haspelmath [@haspelmathWhatConstruction2023]

### Constructions and pragmatics

- ==Add== [@finkbeinerReflectionsRolePragmatics2019]
- Thus CxG should consider semantics as truth-conditional and pragmatics as non-truth-conditional content while additionally distinguishing between conventional and non-conventional pragmatics, i.e. conventional implicatures as part of the CxN meaning vs. conversational implicatures [@leclercqSemanticsPragmaticsConstruction2020]

### Constructions and frames

- Frame semantics is a theory of meaning proposed by Ch. Fillmore
- It is compatible with CxG and is referred to e.g. by A. Goldberg [@goldbergConstructionsConstructionGrammar1995]
- An integrational framework for CxG and frame semantics is proposed by A. Willich [@willichKonstruktionssemantikFramesGebrauchsbasierter2022; @willichIntroducingConstructionSemantics2022]
